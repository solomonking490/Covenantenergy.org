    What this proof of concept shows

    It shows

    The alert logic, escalation rules, dashboard view and six-step response chain (Detect, Verify, Alert, Acknowledge, Respond, Record) work end to end, and what a difference acknowledgement makes when a transformer overheats.

    It does not show

    Sensor accuracy on a real transformer, the false-alarm rate, responder behavior in the field, or cost per site. The planned 20-site, six-month pilot with a licensed operator tests those.

    Mini case study: Idera Oduguyan, Ikorodu, Lagos State

    Between February and August 2026, the community’s local distribution transformer was vandalized twice for the resale value of its copper. In the same period, an undetected fault left it overheating, and it had to be replaced completely. The community levied itself to pay for it all, making electricity less affordable, less available and harder to access. Much of this loss was avoidable with timely information.

    What happened (community account)What TransGuard would show
    Transformer vandalized twice for copperAn enclosure-open and tamper alert to a named responder at the first intrusion. Try “Forced entry”.
    Undetected fault, transformer overheatsAn oil-temperature warning before failure, with time to inspect. Try “Overheating fault”, then do not acknowledge.
    Transformer replaced, community levies itselfEarlier action that can limit loss. The size of that effect is what the pilot measures. It is not assumed here.
